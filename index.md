# IST 597: Advances in Generative AI and Applications

## Overview

This graduate special topics course surveys recent advances in generative AI and focuses on reading, presentation, and project-based learning rather than traditional lectures. Students will study contemporary methods and systems for building generative AI applications, such as large language models, diffusion models, multimodal models, and agents. In this course, students will analyze whether these systems are reliable and trustworthy, and how they are evaluated and deployed in real-world settings. Students will examine common failure modes (e.g., hallucinations, robustness and security issues, privacy risks) and practice strategies for testing, measuring, and improving system performance. The course is designed to support both students aiming for research (e.g., reading papers, developing novel ideas) and students focused on applications (e.g., building and evaluating end-to-end GenAI systems). The course culminates in a substantial team project where students build and evaluate a GenAI system for real problems or conduct a research-oriented study.

## Course Objectives

- Critically read and present SOTA results in generative AI, identifying key claims, assumptions, and limitations.
- Design and implement GenAI applications for real-world problems.
- Develop rigorous evaluation plans for GenAI systems, including task metrics, baselines, and stress tests, and interpret results with appropriate caution.
- Diagnose and mitigate common GenAI failure modes using practical engineering and evaluation techniques.
- Communicate project results effectively through written reports, reproducible code, and presentations/demos.

## Project
Students will work individually or in teams of two to complete a semester-long project of their choice. Expectations for two-person teams will be higher in scope, technical depth, evaluation, and deliverable quality than expectations for individual projects.

### Project Milestones
- Project Proposal: Students will select a specific project topic early in the semester and present their proposal to the class for discussion and feedback.
- Midterm Project Report: Students will submit a report documenting their progress, preliminary results, challenges, individual contributions, and plans for the remainder of the semester.
- Final Presentation: Students will present their completed project and demonstrate the resulting system or findings to the class.
- Final Report: Students will submit a final report in the style of a conference paper or technical system report. The report and presentation will be completed as a team (if working in teams), but each student must clearly document their individual contributions.
 
 
### Project Topics
The course supports both research-oriented and application-oriented projects. Students may select any project related to generative AI, but projects should be interesting, feasible within one semester, and supported by a clear evaluation plan.



 
## Technical Tutorials 
- Each student will conduct a 30-minute technical tutorial on a selected topic in generative AI. A list of suggested topics will be provided, but students will choose the specific materials, examples, and presentation approach used in their tutorial.

- The goal is to teach the class about an important or emerging GenAI topic that goes beyond a general introduction or a summary of a single research paper. Tutorials should explain the relevant technical details, demonstrate how the method or system works when appropriate, and include the presenter’s own analysis, critique, and reflection.

- The presentation format is flexible. Students may use slides, live demonstrations, notebooks, Google Colab, code walkthroughs, or a combination of formats. Presentation materials must be uploaded at least one day before the scheduled tutorial.

- Tutorials may draw from academic papers, technical reports, benchmarks, open-source repositories, industry standards, substantive engineering blogs, documentation, or real-world systems.

## Generative AI Policy
- GenAI Policy: Because this course studies generative AI, students are permitted to use GenAI tools for appropriate course-related purposes, including brainstorming, coding assistance, debugging, information organization, editing, experimentation, and project development. However, these tools may not substitute for a student’s own technical understanding, critical analysis, or intellectual contribution.

- Students remain fully responsible for the accuracy, originality, citations, code, arguments, and results in everything they submit. Students must be able to explain and defend all submitted work. Fabricated citations, unverified claims, misrepresented experimental results, or the submission of substantially AI-generated work as one’s own analysis may constitute violations of academic integrity.
